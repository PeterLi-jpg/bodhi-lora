The authors would like to once again extend their sincerest gratitude to all the reviewers for their incredibly rigorous insights, as well as the AC, as the Area Chair for facilitating these thoughtful discussions. Our responses in whole to the six issues addressed within the meta review are as follows.

Since the reviews we re-ran the pipeline unchanged on three further base models across two more families and two further benchmarks, five seeds per cell, hyperparameters fixed. Active inquiry rises in five of the six added cells we report; the sixth is a documented null with a diagnosis. A seventh, MedQA on Mistral-24B, was set aside as an exam task and is described in our HomM reply.

**W1. MedGemma does not align with HealthBench use cases.** Reviewer FzpA is right. Weight-level adaptation is the object of study so the base had to be open-weight, and that never required a clinically tuned one. On four HealthBench bases active inquiry moves 17.5 to 45.6% (MedGemma-27B), 25.6 to 56.6% (Mistral-Small-24B), 11.8 to 52.5% (Med42-8B) and 11.7 to 10.1% (BioMistral-7B). The general-purpose model FzpA suggested reaches the highest adapted rate of the four.

**W2. One pipeline, contribution stated as a general framework.** You and FzpA identified language that outran the evidence. The contribution will be described as a recipe plus an evaluation decomposition, demonstrated on open-ended information-seeking clinical Q&A. The sentences claiming any CoT protocol or any PEFT method would serve are removed. We tested one CoT protocol in one domain, and generality beyond that is unestablished.

**W3. Automated grading is limited.** The filter and the evaluator sit in different model families, so the metric avoids partly measuring agreement with the selector. That buys independence. Clinical validity is a separate property and the paper will say so. If accepted we add a clinical judge as a robustness panel alongside the primary grader. The three-physician validation is pre-registered in Appendix B and in progress: one rater has returned, kappa 0.35 against the grader, below a 0.6 target.

**W4. Does SFT distill calibration or mimic favourable responses?** Reviewer LxMF proposed the test and we ran it, using labels the grader never sees. Discrimination between information-withholding and self-contained prompts is +2.1pp for the base with the CI containing zero, against +11.0pp for the wrapper and +10.5pp for the adapter, both CIs excluding zero. The direct adapter-minus-base contrast is +8.4pp, CI [-1.2, +17.9], so the size of the gain is unestablished. We present it as consistent with acquired targeting without calling it settled.

**W5. Discussion and contextualization lack clarity.** Accepted. Figure 2 is split so graded dimensions and percentage rates have separate axes, Table 2 gets a direction arrow on every row, and the vague formulations FzpA quoted are replaced with per-dimension numbers and a verbatim example each. One substantive revision: the paper attributes the stacking interference to competition for behavioural capacity. Testing LxMF's alternative showed it tracks format leakage, and the paper will say so.

**W6. No comparison with prior work.** Open. Section 2 positions the method against STaR, Constitutional AI and CoT prompting conceptually. An empirical head-to-head against a published inference-time calibration method is the next experiment.

**What the added data cost.** Aggregate rubric score falls from base to adapter in all six added cells, by 0.013 to 0.015 on HealthBench and 0.016 to 0.086 on the other two, largest on MedQuAD. Paired t-tests over five seeds put all six below p = 0.02, all six surviving Holm correction. The submitted cell, where the adapter came out slightly above base at 0.459 to 0.463, does not represent the pattern. We first read part of the drop as a rubric artefact penalising asking; we tested that and it failed, so we withdraw it, along with a convergence claim computed with the null excluded.

**On Reviewer LxMF's two tests.** LxMF proposed two analyses, on surface mimicry and on the cause of the stacking interference. We ran both and reported them in full. The mimicry test is one of two measures that stand independent of the LLM grader, and the interference test narrowed our own stated mechanism. We have not heard back since, and raise it only so the record shows both were run.

**On consolidation, which Reviewer HomM raised.** If accepted, the camera-ready carries all seven added cells in the main evaluation, the BioMistral null and the MedQA cell among them; the behavioural gain and the aggregate cost stated together for every cell, with the trade-off in the abstract alongside the gain; a Discussion criterion for when the gain is worth the cost, framed by deployment risk; the scope named as patient-facing clinical Q&A; and the interpretations we corrected in this discussion appearing as corrected. That list is on the record in our HomM reply.
